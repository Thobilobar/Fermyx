What the app reads and why

To show Mixer, Devices & Routing, Rules and status, Fermyx reads Windows audio endpoints, default devices, volume and mute states, audio sessions, running application identities, process IDs, executable paths, and some window and foreground-app information. It uses these to display and change routes, apply the rules and profiles you configure, avoid controlling its own processes, and recover audio when a route changes.

If you enable notification access in Windows, Fermyx listens for new notifications to trigger a rule or duck audio. The current listener checks the notification’s originating app identity; it does not read or store message body text. Windows controls that permission. Fermyx can also read the microphone privacy/use indicator and audio capture sessions for applicable rules and recording source choices.

Where a legal basis is required, the local processing needed to provide features you request is based on performing the service you choose; optional recording and downloads occur on your instruction. Local reliability diagnostics and minimal daily installation-presence measurement support the publisher’s interest in maintaining and planning the app; contact the publisher to ask about the legal basis or exercise applicable rights. The rules you configure automate local audio settings; Fermyx does not make decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects.

Audio you choose to process

Live Sound processes selected application audio locally through the Sound Host and optional VB-CABLE driver. Soundboard plays clips locally and can route them into a virtual microphone. Recording starts through a recording action you invoke, including a configured global hotkey for an app; Audio Tools processes files you import. Processing uses memory buffers and local temporary files. The current app does not send audio or recordings to a Fermyx server.

You control whether and where to export recordings, processed files, clips and backups. Other apps can receive audio that you deliberately send through a microphone or shared file. You are responsible for permission to record people and to use any copyrighted content.

What stays on your PC

Fermyx keeps settings, device preferences, app catalog, aliases, profiles, rules, hotkeys, sound settings, soundboard configuration, notification history, and some page activity in its Windows app data/WebView2 storage. Saved clips and intermediate Audio Tools media are under the app data folder; recordings and exports are saved in the folders shown or chosen in the app. The optional separation model, driver download/setup files, signed updater downloads and routing recovery information may also remain locally.

Diagnostics under %APPDATA%\Fermyx\logs can contain timestamps, process and app identifiers, paths, device IDs, session events, and errors. They are not anonymous. The app writes JSONL and readable log files; each of those files rotates at about 5 MB, retaining one rotated copy, while other host logs and local files may remain until you remove them. Verbose logging adds more detail and is off by default.

Activity, retention and deletion

The main Activity timeline holds up to 200 recent entries; its optional history setting controls persistence across restarts. Sound, Soundboard and Audio Tools each retain up to 80 recent page entries locally. The notification panel retains up to 100 entries locally. Clear Activity history also clears these page entries and completed notifications; ongoing operations remain visible. It does not delete diagnostic files, recordings, clips, exports or model files.

You can delete individual recordings, clips and processed items in the app and choose their save locations. Reset to defaults clears custom configuration but keeps recordings, clips, processed files and diagnostic logs. Uninstalling may leave user-created files, old app data, downloaded models and the separately installed VB-CABLE driver. Use Settings → Privacy & diagnostics → Open folder to inspect app data and delete remaining files when Fermyx is closed. Windows backups, copies you made, and files you sent to others are outside the app’s deletion controls.

Network requests and third parties

Signed releases check GitHub Releases for updates unless disabled. In builds with a configured Cloudflare receiver, Fermyx automatically sends a small daily presence summary for each UTC day the app runs, even if both optional reporting switches are off. It includes a random installation ID, a separate random daily ID, first and last observation times, whether someone interacted, the number of launches and app version. The installation ID persists across updates and reporting choices; it identifies an installation, not a verified person. Another Windows user, device or reinstall can receive another ID. You can view and copy your ID in Settings. Keeping the app open across UTC midnight counts the next day. Dates observed offline are queued locally up to 90 days and sent when online. This supports estimates of reach, retention and version adoption. The independent feature/performance and error-sharing switches start off. If enabled, the first sends bounded counts of broad features and aggregate count, total and maximum duration for recording, Soundboard and Sound configuration. The second sends known warning/error codes, broad causes, times and counts. Disabling them discards unsent optional summaries but does not stop daily presence or undo earlier deliveries. Typed feedback is sent only when you submit it; do not include sensitive information. No raw logs, app names, paths, audio or recordings are uploaded automatically. Cloudflare and network providers can see connection metadata such as IP address and time; the Worker does not save IPs in D1. Server summaries and feedback are deleted after approximately 90 days. Builds without a receiver send no reporting data. There is no account or advertising SDK.

If you request VB-CABLE installation, the download contacts VB-Audio; if you request the separation model, the download contacts Hugging Face. Opening vendor or model links opens your browser. These providers and your network provider can see ordinary connection information such as your IP address, time and requested file. Their own privacy terms apply. The app verifies the downloaded package/model before use. Fermyx does not automatically upload your audio to them.

Service configuration

Configured builds periodically fetch a small signed policy from Cloudflare without including the installation ID in that request. It can pause new online downloads or reporting, adjust bounded check/upload intervals and optional sampling, or display a notice. It cannot execute code, change audio controls, start recording or enable optional sharing against your choices. The last verified policy is cached locally; expired policies return to built-in defaults. Daily delivery includes its last verified revision. Processor architecture is included only with optional feature/performance sharing. A random installation credential authenticates adopted records; the server stores only its hash. The publisher inspects summaries and voluntary feedback through an authenticated console. Cleanup removes old rows in bounded batches; outages or backlog can extend retention beyond the 90-day target. Separate backups may expire later.

Device diagnostics, activity signals and targeted availability

From version 1.0.13, basic presence can also include bounded daily totals for interactive, processing, idle, background and unknown states, their original observation times and the last verified rollout revision. These states describe observations, not a guaranteed live connection or a verified person. Sleep, missing observations and delayed offline delivery are not treated as continuous active use. Feature names and detailed device diagnostics remain outside basic presence.

Optional feature/performance sharing can include Windows build, process and native architecture, CPU vendor/model and available logical processors, installed RAM, app/runtime version, app language and runtime/Windows locale, timezone setting and UTC offset; sampled system CPU, owned-process CPU and working sets, available memory, power/battery and uptime; broad audio connection type, format, availability and active/default role; approximate endpoint-use totals; app workload and operation-duration distributions. Endpoint identifiers are transformed into installation-scoped hashes. Personalized endpoint names, raw IDs, MAC addresses, serial numbers, machine/user names, other app names, paths, keystrokes and audio are not automatically uploaded. Error sharing can attach the same bounded technical context to known issue codes, with allowlisted numeric event measurements and original times. Context can be missing or stale and is labelled accordingly. Missing data does not mean a feature was unused or a device was healthy. Linked installation records are pseudonymous, not anonymous.

Detailed collection and delivery follow the independent optional sharing choices; disabling a choice clears its unsent optional summaries. Records observed offline retain their original dates within bounded queues and the existing approximately 90-day retention target. The publisher can compare reported cohorts and publish signed, expiring availability settings or plain-text notices for supported capabilities. Matching can use app version, deterministic rollout percentage, hashed installation targets, hardware bands, language, timezone settings or audio connection type. Matching occurs locally; timezone/locale are not verified geographic location. Policies cannot execute code, alter audio, read arbitrary files or enable disabled optional sharing. Automatic update offers can be staged while manual checks remain available and update signatures remain required. Rollout and consent changes do not erase data already received; contact the publisher for applicable server-data requests.

Choices and Windows permissions

You may leave Live Sound, virtual microphone routing, recording, optional downloads, persistent Activity history and verbose logging off. Change Windows notification and microphone permissions in Windows Settings. The feature/performance and error-sharing switches are independent and off initially; reset turns both off and discards unsent feedback. Daily presence remains on in builds with a reporting receiver, including after reset. The local telemetry-state.json queue is stored in Fermyx app data; removing it deletes the ID and queue locally but cannot retract earlier deliveries. Contact the publisher with your copied installation ID to ask about server records. Manual feedback is a separate action.

Security, children and your rights

Fermyx uses local Windows file and app permissions, checks downloaded model hashes and verifies the VB-Audio installer signature and signed app updates. Files you export or store in accessible folders are protected by your Windows account and any disk security you configure; this policy does not claim end-to-end encryption of local content. Keep your PC and backups secure.

Fermyx is a general audio tool and is not directed at children. Depending on the law that applies to the publisher and you, you may have rights to information, access, correction, deletion, portability, objection, restriction or complaint to a data protection authority. Most current app data is on your own PC; use the controls above first and contact the legal publisher about data it actually receives. Any new account, support, payment, advertising or other hosted service needs a separate policy update before launch.
